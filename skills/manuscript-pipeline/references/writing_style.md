# Writing Style Rules (manuscript-pipeline reference)

## Sentence Structure
- Use declarative (plain statement) sentences throughout. Avoid introducing clauses or lists with colons mid-sentence.
  - BAD: "The reason is metabolic: the majority of organisms prefer glucose."
  - GOOD: "The reason is metabolic. The majority of organisms prefer glucose."
  - GOOD: "The reason is metabolic, as the majority of organisms prefer glucose."
- Do not use em-dashes (—) or en-dashes (–) as clause separators. Rewrite as separate sentences or use parentheses for brief insertions.
  - BAD: "...products—including rare sugars—have been reported."
  - GOOD: "...products (including rare sugars) have been reported."
  - GOOD: "...products have been reported, including rare sugars."
- Dashes in compound nouns (e.g., phosphorylation–dephosphorylation) and number ranges (e.g., 70–110 g/L) are acceptable. (Range = en-dash, per academic-term-rules §8.)

## Section Headings
- Avoid colons in section headings. Use prepositions or conjunctions instead.
  - BAD: "3.1. Xylitol and Xylonic Acid: Near-Industrial Products"
  - GOOD: "3.1. Xylitol and Xylonic Acid as Near-Industrial Products"

## References
- Attach references at the sentence level wherever possible. Every factual claim should have its own citation.
- When drafting, mark unsupported claims with [REF NEEDED] for later verification.
- Include a comment or note with the cited paper's key finding and DOI/link for traceability.
- Body citation cluster ≤ 3; a paragraph that references "Table N" may not use a cluster.

## Reaction arrows in body text
- No `X → Y step` arrow symbol in body text → convert to `-to-` or natural language.
- Arrows inside a Scheme/equation/table figure are allowed.

## Spelling & Units (per academic-term-rules)
- American spelling throughout (titer not titre, optimize not optimise, …).
- Units: slash form (g/L, g/g, U/mL); no superscript ⁻¹ form.
- E-factor: italic *E* only, sEF/cEF roman.

## Tone
- Avoid the "what prior work failed to do" frame → use a "new and previously unaddressed / complementary" frame instead.
